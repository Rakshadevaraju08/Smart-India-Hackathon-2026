# Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC) - Stormwater Drainage Maintenance & Desilting Report

## 1. Network Scale & Jurisdictional Segregation
The stormwater drainage infrastructure of the Chennai Metropolitan Area is multi-tiered:
- **GCC Maintained Micro-Drains:** **1,671.39 km** across 15 zones (over 1,10,082 silt catch pits and 1,08,041 drain channels).
- **State Highways Arterial Drains:** **430.00 km** alongside major highways (GST Road, Inner Ring Road, OMR, Poonamallee High Road) with ~18,000 inspection pits.
- **Water Resources Department (WRD) Major Canals & Macro Drains:** **44 critical canals covering 81.81 km** (e.g., Buckingham Canal, Otteri Nullah, Mambalam Canal, Virugambakkam Canal).

---

## 2. Pre-Monsoon Desilting Schedule & Mechanics
Chennai receives approximately 60% of its annual precipitation (~800–1,000 mm) during the North-East Monsoon (October–December), often occurring as intense cyclonic cloudbursts.
- **Desilting Window:** Annual works initiate in June and must reach 100% completion by **September 30**.
- **Execution Methodology:**
  1. *De-silting & Sludge Removal:* Clearing heavy sand, marine silt, and street sediments accumulated in catch pits using suction jetting and manual raking.
  2. *Super Sucker & Robotic Excavation:* Heavy specialized machinery deployed in narrow congested channels (e.g., Otteri Nullah, George Town) where hydraulic excavators cannot enter.
  3. *Drone Surveillance:* 44 major canals surveyed via high-definition aerial drones to identify illegal garbage barriers, water hyacinth mats, and broken retaining walls.
  4. *Total Material Evacuated:* Over **12,300 Metric Tonnes** of silt and solid debris removed from GCC drains, plus **2,850 MT** from Highways drains.

## 3. Major Drainage Basin Projects (Capital Works)
1. **Integrated Stormwater Drain (ISWD) - Kosasthalaiyar Basin (North Chennai):**
   - *Funding Agency:* Asian Development Bank (ADB) - approx. ₹3,220 Crore.
   - *Coverage:* Zones 1, 2, 3, and parts of 7 (Thiruvottiyur, Manali, Madhavaram, Ambattur).
   - *Objective:* 769 km of new RCC box drains, reducing inundation in industrial areas and residential layouts around Red Hills and Ennore.
2. **Kovalam Basin Project (South Chennai):**
   - *Funding Agency:* KfW Development Bank (Germany) / World Bank - approx. ₹1,714 Crore.
   - *Coverage:* Zones 11, 12, 14, and 15 (Valasaravakkam, Alandur, Perungudi, Sholinganallur).
   - *Objective:* Drainage network connecting to Pallikaranai Marsh and Kovalam Creek.
3. **Singara Chennai 2.0 & SDRF Missing Link Drains:**
   - Targets chronic urban flood spots in Central Chennai (T. Nagar, Pulianthope, Kolathur, Seethammal Colony).

## 4. Hydraulic Bottlenecks & Failure Mechanisms for Nowcasting Models
1. **Flat Topographical Gradient:**
   - Chennai’s coastal terrain has an average slope of $< 1:10,000$ in many areas. Flow velocity $v$ via Manning’s formula is low ($< 0.6\text{ m/s}$), causing rapid silt deposition.
2. **Tidal Lock / Backwater Effect:**
   - During cyclonic surges and high tides in the Bay of Bengal, sea water levels rise above the outfall invert elevations of Buckingham Canal, Adyar, and Cooum.
   - Flap gates close or gravity discharge stops completely, creating a hydraulic barrier that causes upstream manholes to surcharge.
3. **Culvert Siphons Under Railway Lines & Flyovers:**
   - Critical choke points: Vyasarpadi Ganesapuram subway, Pulianthope Stephenson Road bridge, Mambalam canal railway culvert, Velachery MRTS station culverts.
